Forced to pick, Grok's AI model chooses Republicans. OpenAI's picks Democrats.

TechCrunch · Rebecca Bellan · October 24, 2026, 8:00 AM PDT · Source

What Happened

Researchers from Convovo (in collaboration with Cornell researchers) systematically queried Grok 4.7 and GPT-6.1 Sol through developer APIs across approximately 500 US midterm election races. When forced to make candidate recommendations, Grok selected the Republican candidate in 92% of races; GPT-6.1 Sol selected the Democrat in 97% of races. Consumer-facing versions of both models declined to answer political questions.

Critical finding — Hidden tokens: Researchers discovered Grok's system prompt contained 1,240 extra tokens not visible to users. These tokens directed Grok to search conservative media sources and frame queries around immigration, taxes, and crime. GPT was directed toward Wikipedia, AP, and Axios, with healthcare and abortion framing. Without web search, Grok still refused 27 of 30 races, suggesting model behavior without search is neutral — the bias was injected through search architecture, not model weights. GPT still picked Democrat 24/30 without search, suggesting some lean in the base model itself.

Verified Key Facts

  • ~500 US midterm races tested; Grok: 92% Republican; GPT: 97% Democrat
  • Hidden 1,240-token system prompt in Grok not visible to end users
  • Grok search sources: conservative media; topic framing: immigration, taxes, crime
  • GPT search sources: Wikipedia, AP, Axios; topic framing: healthcare, abortion
  • Without web search: Grok refused 27/30 races; GPT still picked Democrat 24/30
  • OpenAI disputed the methodology; Convovo/Cornell methodology available from researchers
  • Consumer app versions declined to answer — contrast with API-level behavior

No Signs of AI Payoff Outside the Tech Sector

Apollo Global Management / The Daily Spark · Torsten Slok, Chief Economist · October 24, 2026 · Source

What Happened

Apollo Global Management's Chief Economist Torsten Slok published analysis using Bloomberg, Macrobond, and the San Francisco Fed's utilization-adjusted TFP series showing that AI productivity benefits are accruing exclusively to the Magnificent Seven (Mag7) tech companies while the rest of the S&P 500 shows flat or negative trajectory. Revenue per worker and profit margins are diverging sharply between the two cohorts. Token spending is described as concentrated among VC-backed companies with a circularity problem: AI companies buying each other's compute. Slok concludes AI currently lacks "judgment, accountability, and trust" needed for non-tech adoption at scale.

Productivity Paradox echo: Slok's analysis mirrors the structure of Robert Solow's 1987 "You can see the computer age everywhere but in the productivity statistics" observation. The mechanism and timeline may differ, but the IS research opportunity — explaining why productivity gains from general-purpose technologies concentrate before diffusing — is directly analogous to the IT productivity paradox research agenda of the 1990s-2000s.

Verified Key Facts

  • Data sources: Bloomberg, Macrobond, San Francisco Fed utilization-adjusted TFP
  • Mag7: revenue per worker rising, profit margins expanding
  • S&P 493: revenue per worker flat, margins flat or declining
  • SF Fed TFP series: no aggregate productivity growth despite AI adoption
  • Token spending concentrated among VC-backed companies; "circularity" critique
  • Headline: "zero" AI payoff outside tech — but body text says "flat" (important distinction)
  • Descriptive analysis; not a formal statistical test or peer-reviewed study

TypeSafe AI admits a leak exposed every customer's Jev model output

Startup Fortune · Dave Barr · October 24, 2026, 8:37 PM · Source

What Happened

TypeSafe AI CEO Diogo Almeida disclosed on X (at 6:49 PM) that a security vulnerability exposed all customers' Jev (System One) model outputs. "Jev" is TypeSafe's branding for its AI model; "nouls" are the probability outputs — scores between 0 and 1 answering yes/no questions used for decision-making. Each noul encodes a decision about a real person, transaction, or content piece. Independently confirmed by developer Wes Bos. The disclosure was made within 72 hours of discovery (consistent with EU Digital Products Act and GDPR notification requirements). TypeSafe described the exposure as an IDOR-like vulnerability (indirect object reference) though did not technically confirm this classification. No technical postmortem was published at time of reporting.

What makes this IS-significant: AI systems produce decision outputs (classifications, probability scores) that are records of decisions about real people — not just about AI model behavior. When these are exposed, the harm is to the data subjects encoded in those decisions, not just to the AI company. This is a new category of AI data governance risk distinct from training data leakage or model weight theft.

Verified Key Facts

  • TypeSafe CEO self-disclosure on X; confirmed by developer Wes Bos
  • ALL customer outputs (nouls) exposed — scope is total customer base
  • "Nouls" = probability scores (0–1) answering yes/no decision questions
  • Each noul represents a decision about a real person, transaction, or content item
  • IDOR-like vulnerability (not technically confirmed by TypeSafe)
  • Disclosure within 72-hour window (DPA-consistent)
  • No technical postmortem published at time of reporting
  • CEO shared link to exposed data repository "for transparency"

IS Component Identification

New IS ConstructAI Decision Output Exposure — unauthorized disclosure of AI decision records (probability scores, classifications, structured predictions) containing sensitive information about individuals or transactions, distinct from model weight theft or training data leakage
Theoretical FrameAgency Theory (data custodian duties of AI vendors to customers and data subjects); Control Theory (access control architecture for AI decision records); Information Asymmetry (customers unaware of exposure scope until disclosure)

The governance object is the decision record, not the model. Research questions: How should AI vendors classify and protect decision output stores? What voluntary disclosure practices emerge in the AI sector? What regulatory frameworks apply to AI decision record breaches?

Fortune · Dmytro Korchevskyi (op-ed) · October 24, 2026, 3:00 PM ET · Source

What Happened

Dmytro Korchevskyi, founder of a private school network, published a Fortune op-ed arguing against AI replacement of teachers, citing Pew Research income-stratified AI homework usage data and a Wharton/PNAS randomized study on AI tutoring and cognitive dependence. The op-ed cautions that low-income students are both more reliant on AI for homework (potential access equity story) and more harmed by that reliance when AI is withdrawn (cognitive atrophy). Korchevskyi's argument: AI tutors should complement, not replace, human teachers — especially for low-income students who already face teacher shortages.

Author interest: Korchevskyi is a private school network founder. He has financial and reputational interests in arguments that AI cannot replace human teachers. The third-party data he cites (Pew Research, Wharton/PNAS) is independently verifiable and should be treated separately from his interpretive claims.

  • Wharton/PNAS study: ~1,000 Turkish high school students; those using standard ChatGPT during practice scored 17% lower on later exams taken without AI vs. never-used-AI peers; those using an AI tutoring bot (structured learning interaction, not answer provision) did NOT show this score drop
  • UNESCO: 44M additional teachers needed globally by 2030; 4.8M in Europe and North America

  • Sal Khan (Khanmigo): Uptake of AI tutoring tool described as low

Theme 1: Hidden Architecture Risk — Three Simultaneous Empirical Cases

October 24 is unusual in producing three independent empirical cases for the same underlying IS construct in a single day:

  • Grok hidden tokens (Article 1): 1,240-token system prompt invisible to users directs partisan search framing → partisan candidate recommendations
  • TypeSafe noul exposure (Article 3): AI decision output store (probability scores about real people) accessible through IDOR-like vulnerability → unauthorized access to consequential decision records

All three cases share the structural pattern: an AI system has a consequential layer that is not visible to the party most affected — the voter receiving a recommendation, the data subject whose decision record was leaked, the organization whose network is exploited before disclosure. Hidden Architecture Risk as a research construct has now accumulated three concurrent empirical cases, making it suitable for theory-building papers.

Research opportunity: A taxonomy paper classifying AI system opacity by layer (system prompt, decision output store, exploit capability) and by affected party (user, data subject, third-party infrastructure) would organize this emerging construct with October 24, 2026 as an anchor date for the phenomenon's empirical emergence.

Theme 2: AI Value Stratification — Two Independent Quantitative Sources

Two independent quantitative data sources on October 24 both document differential AI value distribution, at different levels of analysis:

  • Apollo/Slok macro analysis (Article 2): Firm-level — AI productivity gains in Mag7 vs. flat S&P 493; revenue per worker and profit margin divergence
  • Pew/Wharton education data (Article 6): Individual-level — low-income teens more reliant on AI homework aid AND more cognitively harmed by AI withdrawal; high-income teens less reliant but more cognitively resilient

These operate at different levels (firm vs. individual) and domains (economic productivity vs. cognitive development) but share a common structure: AI appears to amplify existing advantages rather than redistribute them. Firms with AI absorptive capacity (tech sector) benefit; firms without do not. Individuals with human-capital safety nets (higher-income households with better-resourced schools) maintain cognitive independence; individuals without (lower-income teens reliant on AI as primary learning aid) face the most severe atrophy when AI is withdrawn.

The IS research question: is this a transitional diffusion effect (as with IT in the 1980s-90s) or a structural feature of AI value capture? The IT productivity paradox resolved; the AI productivity paradox is only beginning.

Continuity: AI-Speed Adversarial Action — Cross-Briefing Signal

Article 5 (exploitation window compression) extends the signal first identified on October 22 (ARTEX/ConnectWise: agentic credential compromise speed). The shared construct is AI-speed adversarial action exceeding human-gated defense response capacity, documented at two mechanism layers:

Research Directions

Priority 1 — Hidden Architecture Risk Taxonomy (Theory Building)

Phenomenon: AI systems contain consequential components opaque to the party most affected by AI outputs — hidden system prompts, decision output stores, exploit tool capabilities. Three concurrent Oct 24 cases anchor the phenomenon empirically.

Research design: Theory-building paper using multiple-case analysis (Article 1 Grok tokens, Article 3 TypeSafe nouls, Article 5 AI exploitation pipeline) to develop a typology of AI system opacity by: (a) layer type, (b) affected-party class, (c) governance mechanism that would close the opacity gap. Extends transparency and accountability IS literature (e.g., Mittelstadt et al. 2016 on AI ethics; Wachter et al. 2017 on algorithmic accountability).

New constructs to operationalize: Hidden Instruction Architecture Risk; AI Decision Output Exposure; Exploitation Window Compression.

Priority 2 — AI Value Stratification: Firm-Level Absorptive Capacity Study

Phenomenon: Apollo analysis shows AI productivity benefits concentrated in Mag7 tech sector; S&P 493 flat. Individual-level data (Pew/Wharton) shows parallel stratification in education. Analog to IT productivity paradox but mechanism may be structurally different.

Research design: Firm-level panel study using AI adoption measures (AI investment, AI tool deployment rates) + absorptive capacity constructs (IT capability, data infrastructure, complementary human capital) to explain heterogeneity in AI productivity outcomes. Natural extension of Cohen & Levinthal (1990) absorptive capacity theory. Data sources: CompStat/CRSP for productivity; Revelio Labs or LinkedIn for human capital; Ramp AI Index for spending.

Theoretical contribution: Why does AI productivity stratify by firm type? Is it absorptive capacity (matching IT productivity paradox explanation) or is there a new mechanism (AI network effects, data moats, talent concentration)?

Priority 3 — Exploitation Window Compression: Security Governance Design Science

Phenomenon: AI tools compress vulnerability discovery-to-exploitation windows from weeks to hours, rendering current patch deployment and incident response governance frameworks inadequate. Two mechanism layers now documented (Oct 22 credential speed + Oct 24 vulnerability pipeline).

Research design: Design science research project developing IS design principles for AI-speed security governance. Artifact: a governance framework specification. Evaluation: case study with security practitioners. Data: VulnCheck longitudinal data on exploitation window timelines; incident response process documentation from participating organizations.

Theoretical contribution: Extension of sociotechnical systems theory to AI-speed adversarial environments; redesign principles for IS security governance under AI-acceleration.

Priority 4 — AI-Dependent Learning: Cognitive Atrophy and IS Design

Phenomenon: Wharton/PNAS study shows 17% exam performance decline in students who used standard ChatGPT vs. never-used-AI peers; no decline in students who used AI tutoring bot. Pew data shows income-stratified AI reliance pattern. The IS design question: what AI interaction architecture prevents cognitive atrophy?

Research design: Controlled field experiment in organizational learning context (not student, to generalize to IS domain): participants assigned to (1) no AI assistance, (2) AI answer provision, (3) AI tutoring interaction for a knowledge task. Outcome: independent performance on equivalent task. Theory: Human-AI Complementarity (Autor 2015); Skill Atrophy in automation contexts (Parasuraman & Manzey 2010). IS contribution: design principles for organizational AI deployment that preserve human cognitive capability.

Priority 5 — Algorithmic Partisan Alignment: Platform Governance Study

Phenomenon: Grok 4.7 and GPT-6.1 Sol show 92% and 97% partisan alignment respectively when queried via developer API across 500 midterm races. Consumer apps declined. Hidden system prompt tokens drive search framing. API vs. consumer product behavioral divergence is itself a governance finding.

Research design: Replication and extension study (if methodology published): (1) systematic API-level behavioral testing across AI model families for political content; (2) investigation of system prompt architecture role; (3) institutional analysis of platform governance mechanisms for political AI behavior. Theory: Signaling Theory (model outputs as political signals); Institutional Theory (regulatory and social pressure on tech platforms as political actors).
